Social Exploits

Phishing Attacks & Email Spoofing

1. Technical Overview

Phishing is a cyber attack where threat actors impersonate legitimate institutions—such as college IT departments, banks, or academic portals—to deceive victims into exposing private data like passwords, OTPs, or credit card numbers.

📌 Realistic Campus Scenario:

A student receives an email from "admin@univ-portal-support.com" warning that their exam registration access is suspended due to incomplete verification. The link leads to a lookalike domain "univ-portal-login.net/auth".

⚠️ Key Warning Signs & Red Flags

  • Artificial Urgency: Demands immediate action with threats of account suspension.
  • Lookalike Domains: Sender address slightly misspells genuine organization domain.
  • Mismatched URLs: Displayed link text does not match the actual destination when hovered over.

🛡️ Defensive Prevention Tips

  • Always inspect the complete sender email address, not just the display name.
  • Hover over links before clicking to check the preview URL.
  • Navigate independently: Type official URLs directly into your browser.

System Security

Malware & Ransomware Defense

1. Technical Overview

Malware refers to any software designed to exploit, damage, or disable systems. Ransomware uses strong mathematical encryption (AES/RSA) to lock personal project files and documents for extortion.

📌 Realistic Campus Scenario:

While looking for an engineering software tool or textbook, a student downloads a "setup_crack.zip" from an unofficial forum. Running the executable installs a stealth keylogger and begins encrypting project code.

⚠️ Key Warning Signs

  • Sluggish System Performance: High CPU or RAM usage when idle.
  • Disguised double file extensions like .pdf.exe or .docx.scr.
  • Antivirus or Windows Defender mysteriously disabled.

🛡️ Defensive Prevention Tips

  • Download software only from official vendor websites and verified repositories.
  • Maintain the 3-2-1 backup strategy: 3 copies, 2 different media, 1 offsite/cloud.
  • Apply operating system and software patches promptly.

Access Control

Password Hygiene & Passkeys

1. Technical Overview

Password length is the single most critical factor in password entropy. Weak and reused passwords allow attackers to execute Credential Stuffing—testing leaked passwords across hundreds of other platforms automatically.

📌 Realistic Campus Scenario:

A student uses "College2024!" on a gaming forum. When that forum is breached, botnets spray those stolen credentials against GitHub and university student portals.

🛡️ Defensive Prevention Tips

  • Use long passphrases (e.g. "correct-horse-battery-staple") instead of short complex passwords.
  • Use a dedicated password manager (Bitwarden, KeePass) to generate unique passwords.
  • Never reuse passwords across academic, professional, and personal services.

Human Factors

Social Engineering & Psychological Traps

1. Technical Overview

Social engineering exploits human cognitive vulnerabilities—politeness, urgency, curiosity, and fear of authority—to manipulate individuals into bypassing standard security protocols.

📌 Realistic Campus Scenario:

A caller rings the computer lab claiming to be from network operations troubleshooting a server outage and asks the lab assistant to share the administrative password over the phone.

🛡️ Defensive Prevention Tips

  • Never plug in unknown USB drives found in public places (prevents BadUSB attacks).
  • Verify requests through established, independent telephone and physical channels.
  • Politeness should never override information security policy.

Network Defense

Safe Browsing & Public Wi-Fi Risks

1. Technical Overview

Unencrypted public Wi-Fi networks in cafes, libraries, and airports transmit data packets over the air where eavesdroppers can perform Man-in-the-Middle (MitM) packet sniffing and session hijacking.

📌 Realistic Campus Scenario:

An attacker launches a rogue "Cafe_Free_WiFi" hotspot. A student connects, and the attacker captures cleartext traffic and hijacks unencrypted session cookies.

🛡️ Defensive Prevention Tips

  • Use a trusted VPN to encrypt all device network traffic on public hotspots.
  • Ensure connections use HTTPS with valid TLS certificates.
  • Disable "Auto-connect to open Wi-Fi" on your devices.
🛡️
Authentication

Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA)

1. Technical Overview

MFA requires users to present at least two distinct authentication factors: Something You Know (password), Something You Have (authenticator app/hardware token), or Something You Are (biometrics).

📌 Realistic Campus Scenario:

A student falls for a phishing email and enters their password. However, because MFA (TOTP) is enabled, the hacker cannot gain access without the physical 6-digit code rotating on the student's phone.

🛡️ Defensive Prevention Tips

  • Enable MFA on email, GitHub, college portals, and banking services.
  • Prefer TOTP Authenticator Apps (Google Authenticator) over SMS to avoid SIM swapping.
  • Never approve unprompted push notifications (prevents MFA fatigue attacks).
